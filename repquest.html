The Problem

Why this app

Most fitness apps are built for people who already work out. RepQuest is built for people who don't yet, but want to — our target user is a college student in their late teens or early twenties who's never managed to stick with a gym routine before: motivated in theory, easily discouraged in practice by apps that feel like spreadsheets or gyms that feel unapproachable. Our team's vision statement put it plainly: turn fitness tracking into something closer to a game than a chore, so showing up starts to feel like progress instead of an obligation.

  • Gamified Progression — A winding path of levels to climb, not a flat history list.
  • Daily Streaks — Visible momentum that rewards showing up, not just logging data.
  • Unlockable Achievements — Badges tied to real milestones: total workouts, muscle group, streak length.
  • Low-Friction Onboarding — Clean UI, simple navigation, and legible type so first-timers aren't intimidated before they even start.

UI/UX Design

Designing the visual language

Most of my day-to-day work here was visual design — deciding what a piece of state should look like before anyone touched a database.

  • Color-Coded States — Gray = locked, green = unlocked. No text required.
  • Custom Iconography — One icon per workout type (push/pull/legs/rest), reused consistently across the path, workouts, and achievements.
  • Gamification Pattern — A Duolingo-style winding path instead of a flat history list.
  • Design System — One consistent visual language, not each screen inventing its own.

Reflection

Challenges & what I learned

Extra-credit risk analysis, and what actually mattered once we were building:

  • Responsive Design — Mobile-first mindset for a mobile-heavy audience.
  • Scope Management — Sized sprints against real availability, not wishful thinking.
  • Security Awareness — Flagged auth/DB risk early. (The real failure, months later: a free-tier expiration, not a breach.)
  • QA — Wrote UAT cases for error-state handling across onboarding, calendar, and home.
